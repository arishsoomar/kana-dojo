Design language

The shared pieces every screen is built from. When you start building components, these become your theme file.

Charcoal ink, vermilion, and paper. Each color has exactly one job.

Wrong answers use vermilion, the same red as the seals, so red always means "pay attention" rather than "you failed".

Type

Archivo for English and Zen Kaku Gothic for kana. Both are sturdy and geometric, so they look like one voice.

かカ
The か row
Heavy weights for headings, regular for help text. Plaques read top to bottom, like real Japanese signs.

Buttons and tiles

Flat, with a thin border and sharp-ish corners. No 3D edges, no bubbles. Selection is shown by a thicker border, not a fill.

Belts: the whole progression

Each kana's belt is the engine's mastery tier from tierOf(). Row belts take the lowest belt in the row, so one weak kana holds the row back. Dan stripes come later from keeping your speed up over months.

Karasu, your sensei

A crow in a headband. Its expression is feedback, so you feel the result before you read it.

Karasu grows with your rank

The crow's form is your overall belt, so your profile shows how far you've come at a glance. At black belt it becomes a karasu-tengu, the crow-winged mountain spirit that folklore says teaches martial arts.

Earned, not givenEvery unlock is tied to something the engine measured: accuracy, speed, or memory held over time.
Mistakes are correctionNo lives in lessons. A wrong answer gets a memory tip, and the mix-up is saved for a boss later.
Nothing is decorationBelts, plaques, and scrolls are all real progress data drawn as objects.
Games count as trainingEvery answer in every mode goes through the same engine, so playing is practicing.

How to read these. Screens tagged MVP are the first build and match phases 1 to 5 of the roadmap: the learn path, lessons and feedback, the belt grid and kana details, Kana Rain, and the streak. Everything tagged Later is added one piece at a time afterward, reusing the same engine and the same components.

Names like Kenji and Hana are placeholders. Fonts load from Google Fonts, and every illustration here is drawn in code, so nothing depends on image files.
